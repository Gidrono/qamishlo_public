How it works

Three steps from a word to “one more chapter”

  1. 01

    Type a topic — or browse the free library

    One word is enough: sharks, castles, how planes stay up. Or open a course that’s already in the catalog.

  2. 02

    Get a course pitched to the listener’s age

    Qamishlo writes and narrates an 8–10 chapter series with short recaps and a gentle “what happens next?” at the end of each one. Same curiosity, different vocabulary for 5–9, 10–14, or 15+.

  3. 03

    Listen — and ask anything

    Play on the lock screen, in the car, or downloaded for offline. Pause and ask by voice; the answer fits the lesson, then the story picks back up.

Every course is written for one age band — vocabulary, pacing, and safety rules included. Rome for them, Rome for you, then talk about it on the drive home.

5–9

Little explorers

10–14

Sharp and respectful. Real terms, cause and effect, thought experiments that make ideas click. Never talked down to. History without graphic detail.

15+

And one for you

Questions

People usually ask

What ages is Qamishlo for?

Courses come in three bands: about 5–9, 10–14, and 15+ (that’s you). It works for curious kids listening with a parent nearby, and for adults on a commute or walk.

Does my child need their own account?

No. The account belongs to you. They listen on your device, and you choose the age band for what they hear.

How is this different from a study app with quizzes?

Qamishlo is audio-first and zero-setup: type a word, get a short multi-chapter course, listen on the way. There are no quizzes or flashcards. The point is a calm series you can interrupt by voice — for the car, train or walk.

Is the content safe and accurate?

Age bands have their own writing rules, and topics are screened before a course is made. Everything is AI-written, so it can occasionally get something wrong. Listen together when you can, and tell us if anything seems off.

How do voice questions work?

Pause the lesson and ask out loud (or type). Qamishlo answers briefly using the lesson you’re hearing, then goes back to the story. The voice recording is deleted after it’s turned into text.

How long is a course?

Most courses have 8–10 chapters of about 8 minutes each — one chapter per car ride or commute.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Download chapters ahead of time and listen with no signal. Asking new questions needs a connection; offline questions are sent when you’re back online.

Which languages?

Courses can be made in English, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese. The app itself is in English for now.

Is there a screen to stare at?

Barely. Qamishlo is audio-first: start a chapter and put the phone away. It plays from the lock screen like a podcast.

How do I cancel?

Subscriptions are handled by the App Store or Google Play. Cancel anytime in your store account settings; you keep access until the end of the period you paid for.

When can we get it?

Qamishlo is in testing on iPhone and Android. Join the waitlist and we’ll email you when it’s in the stores.
